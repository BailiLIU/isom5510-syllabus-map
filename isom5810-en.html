ISOM5810 · Business Modeling and Optimization

This course is a hands-on workshop in business modeling and optimization with Excel. The syllabus emphasizes building spreadsheet models as digital representations of business situations, using them iteratively to analyze alternatives and make better decisions. The course aims to provide managerial insight, clarify what business problems can be treated quantitatively and what data are needed, and build a framework for larger-scale modeling with more advanced tools.

1 · Spreadsheet Modeling Foundations

The key skill is not “using Excel” in isolation; it is turning a business situation into a model that supports reasoning.

From business story to spreadsheet model

Inputs

Data, assumptions, rates, capacities, costs, demand, timing, and other facts that define the situation.

Decision variables

The choices management controls: how much, where, when, whom to assign, what to buy, or which option to select.

Relationships

Excel formulas encode how inputs and decisions produce outcomes.

Outputs

Profit, cost, risk, service level, cash flow, utilization, or other decision-relevant consequences.

2 · Modeling Logic & Optimization

Objective, constraints, and feasible choices

A business optimization model typically asks: what should we choose, what are we trying to maximize/minimize, and what restrictions must every solution satisfy?

  • Objective: profit, cost, risk, utilization, service, or another managerial target.
  • Constraints: budgets, capacities, timing, compatibility, policy, or resource limitations.
  • Feasibility: a candidate solution must respect all required constraints.
  • Sensitivity / what-if reasoning: change assumptions and inspect how the recommended decision changes.

4 · Decision-Making Mindset

What the course is trying to change in your thinking
  • Extract the information that matters from a messy business situation.
  • Use formulas to make assumptions and relationships explicit.
  • Analyze the business problem rather than simply calculate numbers.
  • Identify sound decisions and understand why they are recommended.
  • Recognize when a problem is suitable for quantitative modeling and what data must be collected.
The syllabus closes with the principle that the goal of business modeling is to make better decisions.
